# The Drowned Belfry — story bible (wordless)

Status: draft v0 (M0). Everything here is told through murals, statue poses, item descriptions
of one or two lines, boss design and level layout. No text walls, no dialogue.

## The seed

A city once stood above the tide, kept dry less by walls than by sound. Its bell-keeper rang the
great tide-bells each evening, and the water — listening, as it had always listened — kept its
distance. The people below never thanked the bells. You do not thank the sun for rising either.

Then came the thief-king, patient and vain, who had learned that the tide could not be bribed
but could be _lied to_. He stole the voices of the bells one winter, one silence at a time, and
wore them like coin. With the bells mute, the sea did not attack; it simply forgot the city was
there — and forgot it slower than an enemy, faster than a friend. The water climbed three steps
and stopped, the way debt stops when the lender dies.

The keeper hung from the great bell's rope rather than watch it happen.

## The present

You are a small masked wanderer, no one's heir, climbing the drowned belfry district with a
nail that used to be a tool. You cannot speak, and nothing here will speak to you. The past is
told in poses: a statue of the keeper with his hands over the bell's mouth; a mural of the
thief-king crowned in small chimes; children's footprints stopped mid-street, going up.

## What the player should feel

1. The water is not evil. It is owed.
2. The thief-king did not win by force. He won by taking the thing the water trusted.
3. The bells are not weapons. They are promises, and every dead thing in this district is a
   promise that was broken in a different way.

## The arc of the slice

The lower district (rooms 1–6) teaches: bells ring, water listens — enemy bells, swinging cages,
the Warden Moss that grew around an un-rung bell it never understood. The upper belfry
(rooms 7–12) reveals: where the bells went. The Bell Thief — gaudy, guilty, dressed in everything
he stole — is not guarding a treasure; he is _wearing an apology_ he cannot bring himself to
return.

The final door of the slice opens with the Tide-Bell, the one voice the thief never dared wear.
The hero rings it once. The water steps back. Not recovers; **stops**. Some debts can only be
answered, never repaid.

## Style rules

- One to two lines max per item description; fragments only.
- No character names in UI. The thief-king's "name" is a sound the bell makes around him.
- Statue poses must read without text: grief, theft, warning, waiting.
